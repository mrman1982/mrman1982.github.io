What an email or IT project can include

A review of accounts and dependencies

Identify the domain registrar, DNS host, mail provider and account owner. For everyday IT work, review the affected devices and software. You approve the proposed changes, subscription costs and access arrangements before work starts.

Clear limits before changes begin

Email subscriptions, extra mailboxes, software licences and third-party charges are agreed separately. Existing provider limits may affect archive imports, calendar sharing or device support. Those limits are reviewed before a migration is promised.

An IT project does not imply unlimited support, 24-hour cover or guaranteed email delivery. Spam filtering and recipient systems are outside a single business’s control. Ongoing assistance and response arrangements are scoped separately. Specialist applications may also need the original supplier’s involvement.

New work is quoted after a short review. Start with one defined improvement rather than an open-ended project.

Common questions

Can I use an email address on my own business domain?

Yes, where you control the domain and have a suitable mail provider. The setup needs the correct DNS records and an active mailbox or alias. Domain ownership alone does not create a working email account.

Do we have to change email provider?

No. The existing setup is reviewed first. Configuration or account changes may resolve the problem. A migration is proposed only when its benefits, limitations, costs and disruption are understood.

Does changing email affect the website?

It can if DNS records are replaced incorrectly. Website and email records often share the same DNS zone. Existing records and dependencies are reviewed so the agreed mail changes preserve unrelated services.

Can you guarantee that our emails reach every inbox?

No. Correct routing and sender authentication are useful checks, but inbox placement also depends on content, reputation and the recipient’s systems. Real sending and receiving tests provide separate evidence and are arranged with permission.
