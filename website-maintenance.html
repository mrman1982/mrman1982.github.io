A website should make the next step clear

An outdated contact page, a broken form or confusing navigation can stop a visitor finding the information they need. Behind the scenes, unclear hosting ownership and forgotten renewal details can make a simple repair harder. A review looks at both the visitor experience and the accounts that keep the site running.

Useful content and page repairs

Update services, opening information and contact details, repair broken links and improve page structure. Make the next action clear on mobile and desktop without adding unnecessary widgets or distractions.

Hosting, domains and launch support

Review where the website is hosted and who controls its domain. Help with an agreed deployment or DNS change, checking website and email dependencies before replacing records.

A manageable maintenance scope

Agree what is checked, what can be updated and how further work is requested. The plan depends on the platform, plugins, hosting and access available rather than an assumed one-size package.

What website work can deliver

An agreed list of improvements

Start with the pages or faults that matter most. We review the existing website and agree the work, expected result and any platform limitations. New pages, integrations or a larger rebuild are scoped separately when needed.

Changes you can review

Where the setup allows, proposed changes are checked in a preview or working copy before publication. The work can include readable content, descriptive page titles, mobile layout repairs, local performance improvements and working navigation.

Checks and a practical handover

The agreed pages, links and enquiry route are checked after changes. You receive a record of the work and any remaining issues. Domain, hosting and website ownership stay clear, with renewal and routine update responsibilities agreed.

Collect only what your website needs

Maintenance matched to your website

An occasional content update, a software maintenance arrangement and emergency recovery are different types of work. Scope, availability and any response arrangements are agreed before support starts. A website maintenance project does not imply round-the-clock monitoring, guaranteed recovery or a search-ranking promise.

Hosting, domains, paid themes, plugins and other third-party subscriptions remain separate costs unless explicitly included. Some platforms or supplier agreements restrict access. If a repair requires specialist development, a new licence or a larger rebuild, that is discussed before further work.

New work is quoted after a short review. Start with one defined improvement rather than an open-ended project.

Common questions

Can you maintain a website built by someone else?

The first step is to review its platform, access, hosting and condition. Work can be agreed where the setup is supportable. Missing source files, restrictive platforms or unsupported components may limit what can be changed.

Can you help with a broken enquiry form?

Yes, a review can trace the form from the page to its receiving service. Submission, provider acceptance and inbox delivery are separate checks. A real delivery test is arranged with your permission, and unresolved issues are explained.

Do I need to move my website hosting?

Not automatically. Existing hosting is reviewed first. A move is proposed only when there is a practical reason, with the domain, email dependencies, costs and recovery route considered before changes.

Can you promise better Google rankings?

No ranking position is guaranteed. Work can improve useful content, page structure, metadata, internal links and technical issues within the agreed scope. Search visibility also depends on factors outside a maintenance project.
