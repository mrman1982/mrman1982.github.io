A clearer plan for the systems your team relies on

Files spread across individual computers, unclear access permissions and backups that nobody has tried to restore can make ordinary work difficult. Buying a new server on its own will not resolve those questions. The first step is to understand who needs access, what must keep running and how you would recover if equipment failed.

Shared files and controlled access

Set up or reorganise shared storage so staff can find approved business files and access only what their role needs. Agree folder ownership, permission changes and what happens when someone joins or leaves.

Servers and suitable self-hosted services

Plan a local server or virtual machine for an agreed business workload. Review compatibility, storage, licensing and remote access before installation. Self-hosting brings control, but it also needs updates, backups and a named owner.

Backups and recovery checks

Review which data and settings need protection, where backup copies are kept and how recovery would work. An agreed restore check can help establish what a backup actually contains and what remains to be covered.

What a scoped infrastructure project can include

A review before equipment is chosen

We identify the users, applications, storage needs, existing hardware and external dependencies. You receive a proposed scope covering the intended result, access requirements, expected disruption and any equipment or licence costs for approval.

Installation with an agreed change window

The agreed work can cover server or workstation configuration, shared storage, virtual machines, user permissions and a suitable backup approach. Existing data is reviewed before changes, with a recovery route agreed where migration or replacement is involved.

Documented checks and handover

The handover records what was installed, who owns the accounts, where key settings are documented and how routine tasks are handled. Checks are matched to the work, such as accessing shared files with the right permissions or restoring an agreed sample from backup.

Keep sensitive information under clear control

An on-site server does not automatically make every connected service private. We review remote access, backup destinations, support tools and any cloud dependencies so you know where data may go. Access is limited to the agreed task and credentials are handled through an appropriate process rather than a general enquiry form.

Infrastructure can support private AI on your own equipment, connections to online AI services or a combination of both. Hardware, access and data flows are reviewed around the workload. Local AI may need different memory and processing capacity, while online integrations need suitable account controls and clear data-sharing boundaries.

Agree ownership and ongoing support

A setup project is different from ongoing managed support. Maintenance, monitoring, backup review and future changes are included only where agreed. Hardware, subscriptions and third-party licences are quoted or approved separately. There is no automatic 24-hour cover or uptime guarantee.

Complex networks, specialist applications and regulated environments may need input from your existing supplier or another specialist. We establish those dependencies during the review. If the proposed work is unsuitable, we explain that before committing to a build.

New work is quoted after a short review. Start with one defined improvement rather than an open-ended project.

Common questions

Can you work with our existing equipment?

Existing equipment is reviewed first. Reuse may be sensible when it meets the workload, support and reliability needs. Any recommended replacement is explained before you approve a purchase.

Do we need an on-site server?

Not necessarily. A workstation, shared storage device, hosted service or a combination may fit better. The choice depends on your applications, data sensitivity, connectivity, budget and ability to maintain it.

Does a backup guarantee that we can recover everything?

No. Coverage depends on what is included, how often copies are made and whether they can be restored. The scope should state the data covered, retention, recovery checks and who is responsible for ongoing review.

Can infrastructure work support a private AI system?

Yes, where the hardware and access controls suit the workload. A review can cover a local assistant or private document pilot alongside storage and server planning. AI answers still need human review.
