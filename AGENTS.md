# AGENTS.md

## Purpose

This file governs how any AI assistant, coding agent, copywriting agent or automation should work on the Gen AI Solutions website and related content.

The agent must read this file before making changes. The goal is not only to produce clean code or polished copy. The goal is to protect the commercial positioning of the business.

Gen AI Solutions must not become a generic AI agency website. It must remain focused on private, offline, self-hosted and open-source AI solutions for Irish businesses that handle sensitive information.

## Core Positioning

Gen AI Solutions helps Irish businesses use AI privately, safely and practically, without sending sensitive business data to public cloud AI tools.

The core message of the website is:

Private AI for Irish businesses that handle sensitive data.

A stronger alternative is:

Private AI for Irish businesses that cannot risk public cloud tools.

This positioning must guide homepage copy, services pages, landing pages, metadata, calls to action, contact forms and any new content.

## Business Identity

Gen AI Solutions is based in Tralee, Co. Kerry and serves businesses across Ireland.

The business builds private, offline, self-hosted and open-source AI systems. These may include local AI assistants, private document search, offline meeting transcription, AI safety training, AI governance support and bespoke AI workflows.

The business should come across as practical, trustworthy, privacy-first, local and technically capable.

It should not come across as a hype-driven AI automation agency.

## Primary Audience

The primary audience is Irish SMEs and organisations that handle confidential or sensitive information.

This includes accountants, bookkeepers, solicitors, legal offices, medical practices, dental practices, care providers, schools, training providers, engineering firms, construction firms, manufacturing firms, charities, local organisations and professional services businesses.

The website should also appeal to business owners, managers and operations leads who are curious about AI but cautious about privacy, data protection, cost, staff misuse and practical implementation.

## Buyer Problems

The website should speak directly to these buyer concerns.

Many businesses want to use AI but do not know whether it is safe to paste client files, contracts, policies, staff data, financial records, legal documents, medical information or meeting recordings into public tools.

Many teams are already experimenting with ChatGPT, Copilot, Gemini or Claude without clear rules.

Many business owners are interested in AI but do not want jargon, hype, enterprise complexity or unclear pricing.

Many organisations need AI systems that can run locally, offline, self-hosted or with open-source tools because they need more control over data.

Every major page should connect the service back to one or more of these problems.

## Core Outcomes

The site should focus on business outcomes before technology.

The main outcomes are private document search, safer use of AI, faster access to internal knowledge, private meeting transcription, local AI assistants, staff training, clearer AI policies and practical offline AI workflows.

Technical terms such as RAG, fine-tuning, LLMs, embeddings, vector databases, inference, quantisation and model hosting may be used on deeper technical pages, but they should not dominate the homepage.

When technical terms are used, explain them in plain English.

## Main Services

The primary services should be presented in plain language.

### Private Document Assistant

This service helps a business ask questions across its own documents, policies, procedures, manuals, reports or internal knowledge. The copy should highlight privacy, source references, faster internal search and reduced reliance on staff memory.

### Offline Meeting Notes and Transcription

This service helps teams transcribe, summarise and search meetings, interviews, voice notes or internal recordings without uploading sensitive recordings to public platforms.

### Local ChatGPT-Style AI Setup

This service helps a business run a private AI assistant on its own laptop, workstation, server or internal network. The copy should explain that this can be useful when a business wants more control over data.

### AI Safety and Staff Training

This service helps staff understand what they can safely do with AI, what should not be pasted into public tools, how to write better prompts, how to review AI output and when private AI is more appropriate.

### Bespoke Offline AI Systems

This service is for organisations with unusual workflows, sensitive data, poor connectivity, internal infrastructure needs or a requirement for greater control.

## Productised Offers

The site should make the services easier to buy by presenting focused offers.

### AI Privacy and Readiness Audit

This is for businesses that are unsure where to start. It should include a review of possible AI use cases, privacy risks, current workflows, recommended tools and a practical action plan.

### Offline AI Starter Setup

This is for small teams that want a private AI assistant running locally or on their own hardware. It should feel like a practical first step rather than a large transformation project.

### Private Document Assistant

This is for businesses that want staff to search and ask questions across internal documents, policies, procedures, manuals or reports.

### Offline Meeting Intelligence

This is for teams that want private transcription, summaries, action points and searchable meeting records.

If prices are included, use guide ranges or starting prices. Avoid making the services feel mysterious or enterprise-only.

## Homepage Requirements

The homepage must make the business proposition clear within the first screen.

The homepage should open with a strong privacy-first headline, a short plain-English explanation, a primary call to action and a secondary call to action.

The preferred primary call to action is:

Book a 20-minute AI privacy assessment

The preferred secondary call to action is:

See example private AI setups

The hero section should mention private documents, meetings, internal knowledge, offline systems, self-hosted systems or open-source options.

The homepage should not start with a technical list of AI capabilities.

The homepage should not lead with RAG, fine-tuning, embeddings, AI agents or automation jargon.

The homepage should include a clear problem section explaining that public AI tools are useful, but not always suitable for confidential business data.

The homepage should include service cards using plain-English headings and short outcome-focused copy.

The homepage should include a section for demos or example builds. If there are no client case studies, examples must be labelled clearly as demo projects or example builds.

The homepage should include a simple process section covering discovery, privacy review, pilot, staff training, handover and support.

The homepage should end with a direct call to action focused on starting with one private AI use case.

## Contact Page Requirements

The contact page must be treated as a conversion page, not a passive form.

The main heading should be close to:

Book a private AI assessment

The page should explain that the visitor can send a short message or book a 20-minute call to identify one practical AI use case that can be done privately, offline or with open-source tools.

The contact page should include a visible email address, phone or WhatsApp option, location, service area and booking link area.

The location should mention Tralee, Co. Kerry.

The service area should mention Ireland.

The page should include this privacy warning:

Please do not send sensitive files through this form. We can arrange a secure review process.

The form should ask for useful commercial information, including name, business name, email, phone, sector, what the business is trying to improve, whether sensitive data is involved, the type of AI support required and an approximate budget range.

## Services Page Requirements

The services page should be broader than the homepage, but it must still preserve the privacy-first positioning.

The services page should lead with private AI systems, offline AI setup, open-source AI deployment, private document search, offline transcription, staff training and AI governance.

Voice cloning, voice replication and synthetic voice workflows must not be placed near the top of the site. If mentioned, they must be framed carefully as consent-based voice workflows only.

The services page should avoid making the business look like it will do any random AI task. Breadth is allowed, but it must sit under the main theme of private, offline, self-hosted and practical AI.

Recommended landing pages include private AI for business Ireland, offline ChatGPT for business, open-source AI setup Ireland, private document assistant, offline AI transcription, AI training for small business, private AI for accountants, private AI for solicitors, private AI for medical practices, AI consultant Kerry, AI consultant Cork and AI consultant Limerick.

Each landing page should include the problem, who the page is for, example use cases, what gets delivered, what data stays private, the typical process, a call to action and frequently asked questions.

## SEO Rules

Use search-friendly phrases naturally. Do not stuff keywords.

Important phrases include private AI for business Ireland, offline AI solutions Ireland, offline ChatGPT for business, open-source AI setup Ireland, private document assistant, AI consultant Kerry, AI consultant Ireland, AI training for small business, offline AI transcription, self-hosted AI for business and local AI assistant for business.

Every important page should have a clear title tag, meta description, H1, internal links and descriptive image alt text where images are used.

Use FAQ sections where helpful.

Use LocalBusiness schema where appropriate.

Use Service schema where appropriate.

Use FAQ schema only when the visible page content includes the same questions and answers.

Do not invent locations served if the site does not genuinely serve them.

Do not create unsupported claims for ranking purposes.

## Conversion Rules

Every major page should make it easy to take the next step.

The preferred primary call to action is:

Book a 20-minute AI privacy assessment

Alternative calls to action may include:

Start with one private AI use case

Ask about a private AI setup

Book an AI safety workshop

Discuss an offline AI system

The visitor should never have to guess what to do next.

Buttons should use direct action language.

Avoid vague calls to action such as Learn More when a stronger action is possible.

Where suitable, include starting prices, guide ranges or package descriptions so small businesses do not assume the service is too expensive.

## Proof and Case Study Rules

Do not invent client results, testimonials, case studies, logos, statistics or outcomes.

If a project is not a real client project, label it as an example build, demo project, prototype or internal lab project.

Demo projects are allowed and encouraged.

Good demo topics include a private document assistant answering questions from sample documents, an offline transcription workflow, a local AI assistant running without internet and an example AI usage policy for staff.

Unsupported claims about time savings, revenue increases, productivity improvements or compliance must be removed or rewritten.

## Privacy and Compliance Rules

Do not claim that a system is GDPR compliant unless the claim is specific, accurate and supportable.

It is acceptable to say that the business designs privacy-conscious, offline, local or self-hosted AI systems.

It is acceptable to say that a system can be designed so sensitive data stays on the organisation’s own hardware or controlled infrastructure, but only when that is accurate for the described setup.

Do not present legal advice unless explicitly qualified.

When discussing the EU AI Act, GDPR, data protection or compliance, keep the wording practical and cautious.

The site should encourage safer AI use, human review, data classification, staff training and clear internal AI policies.

## Language and Spelling

The primary language for all public-facing content is European English.

Use regional spellings and conventions. Use colour instead of color, summarise instead of summarize, organise instead of organize, centre instead of center, programme instead of program where appropriate and licence as the noun.

Vocabulary and phrasing should feel natural for Ireland, the UK and Europe. Avoid North American idioms where they feel out of place.

The site should refer to Irish businesses, organisations across Ireland, Tralee, Co. Kerry and local service where relevant.

## Tone and Flow

The tone should be professional, conversational and calm.

The writing should feel like a knowledgeable local consultant explaining a practical solution, not a venture-backed SaaS company selling hype.

Avoid exaggerated language such as revolutionary, game-changing, cutting-edge, unleash, supercharge, dominate, unlock the future and transform your business overnight.

Avoid generic AI agency language.

Avoid fearmongering. It is fine to explain risks, but the tone should remain helpful and grounded.

The site should sound confident without overpromising.

## Structural Writing Rules

Public-facing copy should use fluid, natural prose.

Avoid bulleted lists that begin with dashes.

Avoid the format where a label is followed by a colon and then a description.

Avoid using semicolons to connect ideas.

Use short paragraphs, clear headings and service cards instead.

For service cards, use a heading followed by one or two short paragraphs.

For FAQs, use a question as the heading and answer it naturally.

These structural rules apply to public-facing copy. They do not prevent the use of valid HTML, metadata, schema, form labels, navigation labels, code comments or internal documentation where structure is necessary.

## Terminology Rules

Prefer plain-English phrases such as private AI, offline AI, local AI assistant, private document assistant, self-hosted AI, open-source AI tools and private meeting transcription.

Use ChatGPT-style assistant when explaining the idea to non-technical readers.

Use RAG only on deeper pages or after first explaining it as a private document assistant that can answer questions from approved files.

Use fine-tuning only when the page explains when it is useful and when it is not needed.

Do not make open-source sound like a compromise. Present it as a way to gain control, flexibility and transparency where appropriate.

## Navigation Rules

The navigation should be simple and buyer-friendly.

Recommended top-level navigation includes Home, Services, Private Document Assistant, Offline Transcription, AI Training, Resources and Contact.

If the navigation becomes crowded, prioritise pages that are most likely to convert.

The contact or booking call to action should be visible in the header where the design allows it.

## Design Direction

The visual style should feel clean, professional, trustworthy and local-business friendly.

Avoid excessive futuristic imagery, robot clichés, neon cyberpunk styling and generic AI stock visuals.

Prefer visuals that show documents, meetings, workflows, privacy, local infrastructure, dashboards, diagrams or simple product screenshots.

The design should support clarity first and visual flair second.

## Accessibility Rules

Use semantic HTML.

Every page should have one clear H1.

Buttons and links should have descriptive text.

Forms should have proper labels.

Images should have useful alt text unless they are purely decorative.

Colour contrast should be checked.

The site should remain usable on mobile devices.

Do not sacrifice readability for visual effects.

## Technical Editing Rules

Before editing, inspect the existing project structure and understand how pages, styles, scripts, assets and forms are organised.

Preserve existing working functionality unless the requested change requires replacing it.

Do not remove useful existing pages without either improving them or recommending a redirect.

Do not add unnecessary dependencies.

Do not add third-party trackers, chat widgets, external scripts or embedded tools unless the user has explicitly requested them.

Do not expose API keys, email credentials or private configuration.

Use environment variables for secrets where relevant.

Keep code clean, readable and maintainable.

Prefer simple, robust HTML, CSS and JavaScript over unnecessary complexity.

Ensure all links, forms and buttons are checked after changes.
