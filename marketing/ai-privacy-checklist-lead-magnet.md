# AI Privacy Checklist For Irish Businesses

## Working Title

AI Privacy Checklist: What Irish Businesses Should Decide Before Staff Use Public AI Tools

## Purpose

This lead magnet should create qualified enquiries from business owners, managers and operations leads who are already thinking about AI but are unsure what is safe.

The checklist should point naturally towards:

- A 20-minute AI privacy assessment.

## Positioning

Use this framing:

Public AI tools can be useful, but they are not always suitable for confidential business data. Before staff paste client files, contracts, meeting notes, learner information, payroll records or internal policies into AI tools, the business should decide what is allowed, what is not allowed and where private AI may be a better fit.

## Page Structure

### Page 1: Introduction

Suggested copy:

AI tools such as ChatGPT, Copilot, Gemini and Claude can help with drafting, summarising and research. The risk starts when sensitive business information is copied into public cloud tools without clear rules.

This checklist is for Irish SMEs and organisations that handle client files, contracts, staff data, financial records, legal documents, learner information, medical information, policies, procedures or meeting recordings.

It is not legal advice and it does not guarantee compliance. It is a practical starting point for safer AI decisions.

### Page 2: What Are Staff Using Today?

Checklist:

- Do you know which AI tools staff are already using?
- Are staff using personal AI accounts for work tasks?
- Are staff using AI inside Microsoft, Google or other business tools?
- Do managers know which workflows involve AI?
- Is there a written list of approved tools?
- Is there a clear process for asking before using a new AI tool?

Notes:

Many businesses do not have an AI strategy, but they already have AI use. Start by finding out what is happening in practice.

### Page 3: What Data Should Stay Out Of Public AI Tools?

Checklist:

- Client files.
- Contracts and legal documents.
- Payroll records.
- Tax and financial records.
- Staff information.
- Medical, care or health information.
- Student, learner or parent information.
- Meeting recordings or transcripts.
- Internal policies, procedures and board documents.
- Trade secrets, pricing, tenders or commercial plans.

Prompt for the reader:

Which of these exist in your business, and who currently has access to them?

### Page 4: Which AI Tasks Are Low Risk?

Checklist:

- Drafting a generic email with no client details.
- Creating a first draft of a public announcement.
- Summarising public information.
- Brainstorming headings or agenda items.
- Explaining a general concept.
- Rewriting non-sensitive text.
- Creating a checklist from public information.

Notes:

Low-risk AI use is still useful. The goal is not to ban AI. The goal is to keep sensitive work out of the wrong tools.

### Page 5: Which Tasks Need Private Or Controlled AI?

Checklist:

- Searching internal policies or procedures.
- Asking questions across approved business documents.
- Summarising sensitive meeting recordings.
- Drafting from confidential internal material.
- Working with client, learner, patient, staff or financial data.
- Building a reusable internal knowledge assistant.
- Running AI in a location with poor connectivity.
- Keeping prompts, documents and outputs under local control.

Notes:

These tasks may be better suited to private, offline, self-hosted or controlled AI systems. The right option depends on the use case, data sensitivity, hardware, scale and support requirements.

### Page 6: Staff AI Rules

Checklist:

- Staff know which AI tools are approved.
- Staff know what data must not be pasted into public tools.
- Staff know when to redact or generalise prompts.
- Staff know that AI output must be reviewed.
- Staff know who to ask before using AI with sensitive information.
- Staff know when private AI may be more appropriate.
- Managers review AI use regularly.

Practical policy starter:

"Do not enter client files, staff data, financial records, medical information, learner information, confidential documents or meeting recordings into public AI tools unless the use has been approved."

### Page 7: First Private AI Use Case

Ask the reader to choose one:

- Private document assistant for approved internal files.
- Offline meeting notes and transcription.
- Local ChatGPT-style assistant for a small team.
- AI safety training for staff.
- AI Privacy and Readiness Audit.

Decision prompts:

- Which workflow wastes the most time?
- Which workflow involves sensitive data?
- Which workflow is repeated often?
- Which workflow would be useful if tested safely with sample data first?
- Who needs to approve the first step?
