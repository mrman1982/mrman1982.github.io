# LinkedIn 30-Day Content Plan

## Goal

Use LinkedIn to create useful business conversations with Irish organisations that handle sensitive data.

The aim is not broad AI commentary. The aim is to make the right people think:

"We should ask Gen AI Solutions about safer AI use before staff go any further with public tools."

## Posting Principles

Stay practical, local and privacy-first.

Use plain English.

Write for business owners, practice managers, operations leads, school leaders, training managers and office managers.

Do not invent proof, client stories or statistics.

Label demo work clearly as demo or example build.

Avoid generic AI agency language.

End most posts with a low-friction CTA:

"If this is on your mind, request a 20-minute AI privacy assessment."

## Weekly Rhythm

Post four times per week:

- Monday: practical privacy or staff-use question.
- Tuesday: sector-specific post.
- Wednesday: short demo or example build.
- Thursday: offer or objection post.

Use Fridays for direct outreach and replies rather than posting for the sake of it.

## Content Themes

### Theme 1: What not to paste into public AI tools

Use concrete but non-sensitive examples:

- Client files.
- Payroll records.
- Contracts.
- Staff data.
- Medical or care notes.
- Student or learner information.
- Meeting recordings.
- Internal policies.

### Theme 2: Start with one private AI use case

Make AI feel manageable:

- One document set.
- One meeting workflow.
- One staff training session.
- One local AI assistant.
- One policy review.

### Theme 3: Private document assistants

Explain the idea without jargon:

A private document assistant lets staff ask questions from approved documents and see where the answer came from.

### Theme 4: Offline meeting intelligence

Explain why meetings need care:

Recordings and transcripts can contain sensitive information. A private workflow may be more suitable than uploading everything to a public platform.

### Theme 5: AI safety for staff

Position training as practical, not theoretical:

Staff need examples, red lines and review habits.

### Day 5

Topic: Solicitors and legal offices.

Post:

The first question is:

What information must not go into a public AI tool?

Client correspondence, case notes, contracts and internal templates all need care.

A private document assistant for approved internal documents is very different from pasting confidential material into a public chat window.

Post:

An AI policy does not need to be 40 pages to be useful.

For many small teams, the first version needs to answer:

What data is not allowed?

When must output be reviewed?

Before rolling out AI tools, ask:

Would every staff member know what not to paste into a public AI tool?

If not, training may be the best first step.
